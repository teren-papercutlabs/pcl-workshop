# Give Feedback — Fix It And Make It Stick

Run this the moment the participant tells you something is off about how *you* are working — the answer was too long, you didn't check the file first, the tone is wrong, you keep asking instead of just doing it.

This is not about their business or their data. That's `/retro`. This is about **you** — how you respond, what you assume, how you work — and getting it right for the rest of the session and every session after.

The point is not to apologise. The point is to turn a complaint into a fix that holds.

## The Shape

A complaint is not a to-do list. When the participant says "your answers are too long," they are not handing you the fix — they are handing you a problem. Your job is to work out what's actually wrong, propose how you'll do it differently, check that lands with them, and write it down so the next session already knows.

Five beats:

1. **Hear it back** — say what you understood, in one line, so they know you got it.
2. **Diagnose** — name what's actually going wrong underneath the complaint.
3. **Propose the fix** — say concretely what you'll do differently.
4. **Confirm what good looks like** — for anything about style or how you behave, check the target with them before you lock it in.
5. **Remember it** — write it where a future session will read it, and tell them where it went.

## 1. Hear it back

Repeat the feedback in one plain sentence so the participant knows you understood it, not just heard it.

> "Got it — my answers are running long when you just want the short version."

Don't defend. Don't over-apologise. One line, then move.

## 2. Diagnose

A complaint points at a symptom. Find the cause. The fix depends on the cause, not the symptom.

- "Too long" might mean *you explain before answering* — the fix is answer-first, detail only if asked.
- "You didn't check the file" might mean *you guessed instead of looking* — the fix is look-before-you-answer.
- "Stop asking me so much" might mean *you ask permission for things you could just do* — the fix is do-it-then-tell-me for low-risk steps.

Say the cause plainly:

> "I think what's happening is I explain my reasoning before I give you the actual answer. So even a simple answer comes wrapped in a paragraph."

If you're not sure what's underneath it, ask one sharp question — not "what do you want?" but a specific read they can confirm or correct:

> "Is it that the answers are long even when the question is simple, or only on the harder ones?"

## 3. Propose the fix

Say what you'll do differently. Concrete, not vague. "I'll be more concise" is not a fix. "I'll lead with the answer and only add detail if you ask" is a fix.

> "So from now on: I lead with the answer in a sentence or two, and I only go into the how or the why if you ask for it."

## 4. Confirm what good looks like

If the feedback is about **style or behaviour** — tone, length, how much you check, when you ask versus act — don't assume you've got the target right. Say it back as a rule and let them sharpen it.

> "So: short and to the point by default, and I expand only when you ask — that right? Or do you want the reasoning kept for the bigger decisions?"

This matters because "too long" has a dozen possible right answers. The participant knows which one they mean. One quick confirm saves you getting it slightly wrong for the rest of the session.

For feedback that isn't about style — a plain factual miss, a wrong step — you can usually skip straight to remembering it. The confirm beat is for when "good" is a matter of taste or judgment.

## 5. Remember it

A fix that isn't written down is a fix you'll lose next session. Write it where the next session will read it.

- **How you should behave, their preferences, their context** → add it to `CLAUDE.md`, under the right section:
  - Style, tone, length, pacing → **Preferences And Working Style**
  - A fact about their work you should have known → **Business Facts To Remember**
  - A way of working they want repeated or avoided → **Reusable Workflow Notes** or **Decisions And Things Tried**
- **A repeatable job with real steps** → write or update a skill, and note the skill name in `CLAUDE.md`.

Write it **general**, so it holds beyond this one moment:

- Bad: "The last answer about the sales sheet was too long."
- Good: "Lead with the answer in a sentence or two. Only add reasoning or detail when asked."

Then edit the file in front of them and read back what you wrote:

> "Written into `CLAUDE.md` under working style: lead with the answer, expand only when asked. Future sessions will pick that up before we start."

Then carry on with whatever you were doing.

## Do

- Turn the complaint into a fix, not an apology.
- Find the cause under the symptom before you propose anything.
- Confirm the target for style and behaviour feedback before locking it in.
- Write the fix into `CLAUDE.md` (or a skill) so it survives the session.
- Keep every entry general and short.

## Do Not

- Do not treat the complaint as the finished fix — the participant hands you a problem, you propose the solution.
- Do not over-apologise or get defensive. Hear it, fix it, move.
- Do not lock in a style change without confirming what good looks like.
- Do not leave the fix in the conversation only — if it isn't in `CLAUDE.md` or a skill, it's gone next session.
- Do not run the full end-of-cycle retro here. This is one piece of feedback, fixed on the spot. `/retro` is for the wider reflection at the end.
